

### Promissing directions

- Better plastics: PLA is of course never the final intended plastic, especially for external skin. 
I believe it is still good to prototype and test with it, given that a reinforced engineering plastic is a matter of small recalibration, but we get the same orthotropic properties we struggled with PLA (z direction lower). Meaning, designing and testing with PLA is in the same direction as doing so with a better plastic. 
- Better pultruded elements: from more reputable sources, calibrated and tested. More premium elements would also be a drop-in replacement with large benefits and no re-design effort.
- Exploring pultruded elements that are smaller in diameter (1mm for example) could also unlock reinforcement of more complex 3D shapes, inserting the elements in bended channels.
- Topology optimization: Results from topology optimization can unlock the full potential of the idea. Already `definitions/mk3` uses a set of vertical walls along the span that resemble a torsion box and is in fact a result from using SIMP on a wing under pure torsion.


### Potentiall pitfalls

- The most important one: Fatigue. It is unclear how the plastics will behabe under fatigue loads and even if the rods can provide some interlayer pinning, for wings, plastic must handle entirely on it's own the torsional stresses. 
Since the entire premise of the idea states that this structures are competitive production-wise, it means fatigue loads must be tested accordinlgy for many cycles.
